#+TITLE: No Cause Found
#+SUBTITLE: Scene 002 — drafted 2026-09-05

* Card

- *Question:* What did Leina think her mother's madness was, and what was it
  actually?
- *Answer:* She thought it was weather. She ran a two-year study with the wrong
  variable in it — because at five she was taught to always form a hypothesis, and
  nobody ever taught her that you can hold the right method and still be pointed
  at nothing. It was grief, arriving by post, on a schedule. And a chain she had
  watched her mother check ten thousand times and filed as a tic.
- *Period:* Leina's childhood.
- *Tone:* Dark.
- *Setting:* The Grey kitchen, Blackmoor. Leina is ten.
- *Characters:* Leina Grey, Nume Moore Grey.
- *Payoff:* She finds Nume's ledgers years later. The dates match.

* Scene

The Weekly came on Thursdays and was worth exactly nothing, which was why Leina
liked it. Grain prices, a boat lost, two marriages, a column by a man in Westhaven
who believed the rain was a punishment and had believed it for six years. Nume
read it front to back every week without ever once saying why.

Leina was doing her figures at the other end of the table and did not look up when
her mother stopped turning pages.

She noticed it the way you notice a clock stopping. Not a sound. The absence of
one.

She kept her pen moving. That was the first part of it: never let her see you'd
clocked it. If Nume knew you'd noticed she would find something for you to have
done wrong, and it was better to be doing figures than to be explaining figures.

"What's the twentieth word."

"Mother?"

"On the page you had open this morning. The Turan. Twentieth word."

Leina put the pen down. She had read that page at half past five and had not been
told to hold it.

"/Hetaira/," she said. "No. /Hetairos./"

"Which."

"I don't—"

"Which."

"I don't know," Leina said.

Nume slapped the table so hard the inkpot jumped and Leina's arithmetic went in a
black flower across the page, and then she was up and out of the chair and across
the room and it was not the first time.

Leina did not move. Moving made it longer.

Her mother stood at the shelf with her back to the room. She put two fingers on
the spine of the Manual — the low shelf, third from the end, where it had lived
since before Leina was born — and left them there. Not taking it down. Not opening
it. Just touching it, the way you'd put a hand on a gate you'd already latched.

Four seconds. Leina had timed it before. It was always four seconds.

Then Nume came back and sat down and said, "You will hold every page you read.
Every page. I don't care what else you are doing," in a voice that had gravel
through it, and picked the Weekly back up, and turned the page she had stopped on.

Leina got a cloth and mopped the ink.

Later, when her mother had gone out to the forge, she went and looked.

Page four. Notices. Six of them, all the same size, all the same dull font, births
and deaths and a mare for sale.

/VELL, Idony. Of Coldhurst. Fever. Aged forty-one./

Leina read it three times. It meant nothing to her. She had never heard the name
in her life. Coldhurst was eleven days away and she had never heard of that
either.

She went back to her figures.

That night she got out the small book she kept under the boards — not a diary, she
had no use for a diary — and made her entry, because she had been keeping the
record for two years now and she was not going to stop just because it wasn't
working.

She had columns for it. /Weather. Slept. Ate. Forge or no forge. Wine./ She had
been trying to find the thing that set them off since she was eight, because if
you could find the thing you could avoid the thing, and if you could avoid the
thing you could have a mother.

The weather column was nearly full. It had never once predicted anything.

She wrote the date. She wrote /rain, as usual./ She wrote /ate — some./ She
considered the evening, and after a while she wrote /no cause found/, which is
what she wrote most weeks, and closed the book, and put it back under the board.

She never thought to write down what day the Weekly came.
