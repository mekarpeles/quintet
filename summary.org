#+TITLE: Quintet — Outline
#+SUBTITLE: Plain plot outline, full spoilers. 2026-09-14.

* What you need to know

- The world is Orbis, pre-industrial.
- "Soul speaking" is a real practice: influencing living things directly. Almost
  nobody understands it. Every household owns a copy of the Soul Speaker's Manual
  and follows its procedures without sensing anything, the way people follow folk
  remedies.
- A rare minority can actually sense feedback when they practice.
- A much rarer few can invent new technique. They are called Orackai. There are
  almost none left.
- The Oracle is a huge formation of black stone. When a living thing dies its
  living force is drawn into the stone and held there — closer to a tree
  sequestering carbon than to an afterlife. Whether anything of the individual
  persists is a matter of belief, and people do believe it.
- The Red Shepherds, led by the Protector, control the Oracle and hunt Orackai.
- A hidden Order protects one accurate copy of the Manual, compiled over four
  centuries. Several members hold decoy copies. Almost nobody knows which is real.

* Book I — The Academy

1. Leina Grey, twelve, lives outside Westhaven with her uncle. Her mother Nume is
   secretly the Order's keeper of the real Manual. Nume has spent twelve years
   forcing Leina to memorise it, and has trained her to appear unremarkable so
   that nobody looks at their family.
2. Leina's father Alistair has spent twenty years working inside the Red
   Shepherds, running their search for the Manual and deliberately steering it
   away from his family. He is killed or removed.
3. The Reds reassign the search to Ardovus Retik, an assassin with a fatal heart
   condition. The Reds treat his heart once a month, and continue doing so only
   while he produces results.
4. Ardovus traces the Manual to Nume and kills her.
5. Leina returns home and is caught by him. She senses his heart condition and,
   improvising from the Manual, binds her life to his: if she dies, he dies.
6. They travel together under a standoff. He still needs the Manual. She needs to
   survive and cannot be killed by him.
7. Reading the Manual properly for the first time, Leina finds a section she had
   memorised as medical case notes. It is actually her mother's record of Order
   members who have been taken or killed.
8. One entry leads her to the town of Coldhurst, where she meets Odile — her
   mother's former teacher and a senior figure in the Order.
9. Odile sends one member of the Order to travel with Leina. This is her first
   student.

* Book II

1. Leina begins a school, recruiting people who can sense feedback but have never
   been trained. These people are invisible to the Reds, who only hunt known
   practitioners.
2. She teaches them her mother's method: form a hypothesis, test it, record the
   result. Nobody has applied this to soul speaking before.
3. It works. She demonstrates that Orackai can be trained rather than born.
4. This puts her in conflict with the Order, whose policy for four hundred years
   has been secrecy and no expansion.
5. The Reds send an infiltrator, presented as an exceptionally gifted recruit —
   possibly another Orackai. This is the one kind of person Leina will not turn
   away.
6. She admits the recruit over the Order's objection.
7. The school is exposed.

* Book III

1. The Protector has spent decades implanting oracle stone and taking lives, and
   is now effectively fused with the Oracle. The two together function as a single
   weapon.
2. The Academy has reached a size where staying still guarantees discovery, and
   the alternative — permanent hiding and movement — has no long-term future
   either. Every remaining path ends in contact with the Reds.
3. Leina does not surrender. She concludes that if contact is certain, the only
   variable left is whose terms it happens on, and moves toward the Protector
   deliberately — defensively, to break the Academy out of a fatal standstill, and
   offensively, to get within reach. The terms she gets are not good. They are
   marginally better than being found.
4. She also concludes the weapon cannot be captured or used, only destroyed. Her
   intent is to bind the Protector to herself as she bound Ardovus, then take her
   own life and remove them both.
4. She fails. The Protector overwhelms her and commits her name to the Oracle.
5. The binding did partly succeed — and it also caught Ardovus, who is still bound
   to her.
7. The Protector, misreading Ardovus as it misreads everyone, offers him
   restoration and a reward for having delivered Leina. The offer is sincere and
   deliverable. Ardovus could take it, and could plausibly survive on his own
   terms even without it.
8. He refuses and attacks, knowing he must die in the exchange. The Protector
   destroys him — and because Ardovus is bound into the thing now bound to the
   Protector, destroying him is the act that ends it.
9. The Oracle becomes unstable. The Protector's city collapses. Leina survives.

* Threads resolved across the three books

- Alistair held his position inside the Reds for twenty years, in collusion with
  Odile, and holding it required producing results. The results were sometimes
  members of the Order. This was the cost of the job rather than a choice he made
  about particular people, and it is not clear how much say he had in any of it —
  including to him. Their purpose was not only to protect Nume and Leina but to buy
  Leina time to develop. He never explains himself and dies without doing so.
  Leina assembles a partial case and is never certain of it.
- Odile has publicly maintained for twenty years that Alistair betrayed the Order,
  including to Leina.
- Some of the names in Nume's record are people Alistair's position cost.
