#+TITLE: Three Scenes — the bind and after
#+SUBTITLE: Unconfirmed, drafted 2026-09-16.

* The Terms
- *Q:* What does Leina buy in the first five minutes, and with what?
- *A:* Time, and she buys it with the only asset she has that he can verify on the
  spot — accurate information about his own body. She never once answers whether
  the bind can be undone.
- The Grey kitchen. Minutes after.

He had gone down on one knee, which she had not expected, and had one hand flat on
the floorboards.

"What did you do."

"Don't get up yet," Leina said. "You'll be sick."

He got up. He was sick.

She waited. Her mother was four feet away and Leina had arranged, in her head,
during the ninety seconds she had spent deciding to do this, that her mother was
not in the room. It was not going to hold. It would hold for an hour.

"Tell me what you did."

"I tied my life to your heart." She kept her hands at her sides where he could see
them. "If I stop, you stop. Not straight away. I don't know how long the gap is."

That was true, and it was a mistake, and she watched him take it and file it, and
made a note to stop being accurate about her own ignorance.

"Undo it."

"No."

"Can you undo it."

"That's not a question you get for free."

He looked at her for a long moment, and she understood two things at once: that he
was working out whether she was worth more than the book, and that he had not
finished being frightened, and that the second thing was the only reason she was
still talking.

She spent it.

"Your heart doesn't fail evenly," she said. "It's worse in the left side than
they've told you. You get it at night, an hour or two after you lie down, and
you've been telling yourself that's the damp."

He did not answer.

"You wake up and you can't fill your chest and you sit on the edge of the bed
until it passes and you have never once told them, because if you tell them, your
price goes up and your use goes down."

"Who told you that."

"Nobody told me that. I can hear it." She let that sit exactly as long as it
needed to. "They can hear it too, if they bother. They haven't bothered. I did it
standing here with a knife at my throat."

Ardovus wiped his mouth with the back of his wrist.

"What do you want."

"Three days. Food, and not to be tied up at night, and you don't send word yet."
She did not say /please/, and she did not say /and then you can have the book/,
because she did not intend to give him the book and there was no sense in starting
with a lie he would remember later.

"And after three days?"

"After three days we'll both know more than we know now," Leina said, "and one of
us will make a better offer."

He laughed. It was one syllable and it sounded like something coming loose.

"All right," he said.

She did not let herself sit down until he had gone out to the yard, and then she
sat down on the floor with her back against the cupboard and looked at her mother
for the first time, properly, and stayed there for eleven minutes, and then got up
and started packing.

* The Test
- *Q:* What does Leina do when she does not know the rules of the thing she made?
- *A:* What her mother trained her to do. Forms a hypothesis and tests it on
  herself, and records the result, and recognises halfway through whose method
  this is, and does not stop.
- Four days out. A barn off the Coldhurst road.

She started a new book on the second night. She had nothing to write on but the
endpapers of a dictionary, and she wrote small.

/What is known:/ he feels it when it happens. It took him at the wrist and the
jaw. He has not had an attack in four days, which is two days longer than he says
is usual.

/What is not known:/ everything else.

On the fourth day she cut her hand.

She did it properly — on the pump handle, where a splinter would have been
reasonable, in the morning, in front of him, and not badly. A finger's length
along the base of the thumb. It bled a great deal, which she had counted on,
because a lot of blood from a small cut is the most convincing injury there is.

Then she watched his face while she wrapped it.

Nothing. Not a flicker.

She wrote: /small injury — no transfer observed. Either the link is not
continuous, or it is below his threshold, or he conceals well. Cannot separate
these./

That night she slept badly and woke at some hour with the cut hot and thudding,
and lay still, and listened to Ardovus across the barn turn over twice, and then
get up, and stand for a while in the dark doing nothing at all.

She wrote in the morning: /larger signal at night. Possibly infection, possibly
fatigue. He was awake. Does not prove anything./

And then she sat with the pen over the page and did not write the next line for
about a minute, because the next line was going to be a plan for a bigger test,
and she had just heard, quite clearly, in her own head, her mother's voice saying
/and what does that prove?/

She thought about what her mother would have done with a subject she could not
question and an instrument she could not calibrate.

Her mother would have increased the dose.

Leina looked at her hand, which was hers, and at the barn, which was cold, and at
the man across it, who had killed her mother nine days ago and could not kill
her.

She wrote: /increase severity. Controlled. Not the hands./

* What She Doesn't Take
- *Q:* What is the coldest thing Leina does in Book I?
- *A:* She goes back for supplies, Melinda finds her, and she does not explain,
  because explaining is kind and kindness would make it a conversation.
- Blackmoor, at night. Eleven days after.

She came in over the back field because the road had a man on it, and she had
ninety minutes and a list.

Melinda was sitting on the step of Haesius's woodstore in the dark, and had
obviously been sitting there for a while, and stood up so fast she knocked the
lantern over.

"I *knew* it—"

"Keep your voice down."

"I knew it, I said to Ma, I said she'll come back for her books—" Melinda got the
lantern up. Her face was doing something Leina did not want to look at. "Everyone
says you're dead. Lemeus says you're dead. I've been sitting out here since —"

"How long."

"Since the fourth night." She said it as though it were nothing. "I brought bread
out most nights in case."

Leina stood in the dark with her list in her head, and eleven of the fourteen
items on it were in the woodstore behind this girl, and the ninety minutes had
started.

"I'm going," she said. "Tonight."

"Where?"

"North."

"All right." Melinda was already nodding, already gathering herself, already
halfway into it. "I'll want an hour. Ma won't notice till Thursday and by then
we'll be—"

"No."

Melinda stopped.

"You're not coming."

"I don't mind it being rough, I've slept rough—"

"You'd slow me by a third. You can't read a map, you can't lie to a stranger, and
you eat." Leina heard her own voice doing it and did not stop. "And the man I'm
travelling with kills people for money, and the first day you annoy him he will
work out that you matter to me, and then you will be the thing he uses. That is
what you would be. Not a friend. A handle."

Melinda's mouth was open.

"Go home."

"Leina—"

"Go *home.*"

She could have explained. It would have taken four minutes, and Melinda would have
understood about a third of it, and would have argued with the other two thirds,
and the ninety minutes would have become forty, and at the end of it Melinda would
still have followed her out of the field.

Cruelty was faster. It was always faster. Her mother had known that too.

Melinda went. She did not run and she did not say anything, which was somehow
worse than either.

Leina loaded the pack in the dark, alone, and it took her longer than it would
have with two people.

She was most of the way across the field when she registered that there was bread
in the woodstore, wrapped in cloth, going stale, seven nights of it.

She did not go back for that either.

* Like a Dog That Dreams
- *Q:* When does Leina learn that Ardovus can feel her, and what does it cost?
- *A:* Seven weeks in, from a complaint. Concealment stops working forever, which
  leaves one option: stop concealing what she feels and start reducing it. The
  bind does not teach her to lie better — it forces her to become, genuinely, the
  thing she had only ever performed. Which is how she becomes a person who can
  walk toward the Protector.
- A let room above a tannery, seven weeks out. Night.
