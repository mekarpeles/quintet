Everything else in storyboard.org was superseded or explicitly killed (the

Note it predates almost all settled canon — the Order, the Protector's nature,

* The Trickster: Wick

** Who he is

No surname offered; he has used eleven. Looks seventeen, claims twenty-four,
unknowable. Freefolk by birth, Wess-trained, loyal to no map. He is the reason
the Grey family library is the most eclectic collection in Blackmoor: for nine
years he has sourced obscure Wesslaki-script catalogs, foreign dictionaries, and
banned treatises, and moved them to Nume through Finn — without ever once
meeting the Greys, and without ever once asking for payment.

Nume Moore Grey, who trusted no one alive, ran a supply line through a
professional liar for a decade and never knew his name.

** His gift: signature forgery

The Reds are learning to read soul signatures. Wick has none that anyone has
ever successfully read.

He cannot heal. He cannot bind. He cannot do a single thing Leina can do. He can
only *copy*. Given a sample — a hair, a used razor, a well-worn tool, a drop of
blood — he can wear another person's signature the way you'd wear their coat.
Under a Collector's sweep he reads as a dead tanner, a Turan customs clerk, a
child who drowned in Westhaven harbor last spring.

His theory: he was born too near the Oracle and it took his signature as a
tithe. Dr. Yitna's theory, offered once and never repeated: he sold it.

** His cost

A worn signature bleeds. Wear a man long enough and you begin to want what he
wanted. Wick has been many people and has real trouble locating what *he* wants.
He makes this a joke roughly once a chapter, and it is the single truest thing
about him.

It is also why Leina cannot recruit him. Enrollment requires a self.

** Why she loves him

- He is the only living person who is not afraid of her, not impressed by her,
  and not asking her for anything.
- He makes her laugh. She experiences this as a catastrophic loss of control and
  craves it like a drug. She was never raised to know a concept of fun; he is
  fun with a knife in it.
- He is the only one who tells her when she is being a monster, and he does it
  as a joke, which is the only format in which she can hear it.
- He never once asks her to be less.

** Why she hates him

Nume's law, taught at five, load-bearing for the entire quintet: *never act
without a hypothesis of the other party's intent.*

Wick has no determinable intent. He is the one problem her mother did not equip
her for. Leina hates him because he breaks her mother. She loves him because he
breaks her mother.

Also: he steals from her constantly and returns everything, always with a lesson
she did not request. He treats the Academy — her life's work — as a bit, while
being more useful to it than anyone. And he knew her father.

** The debt

Wick never takes coin. Only "a favor, unnamed, to be called." He has hundreds
out across three nations. By the end of Book II it is established he has never
called a single one.

Because he isn't collecting leverage. He is maintaining a network of obligations
— the same decentralized structure Leina invented for Smithens and tattooed onto
her Academy. Wick has been running Leina's architecture, alone and badly, since
before she was born.

He inherited it from Alistair Grey, who paid him once, years ago, in a currency
he will not name, to keep the Grey library fed and the Grey girl breathing.

This is the cruelty that rhymes with Nume: Leina can never know whether any of
the kindness was real, or whether she has been receiving an invoice her whole
life. The answer is probably both. They always are.

** The tattoo

When the Academy's induction ritual begins, Wick puts a point on his own forearm
and draws no line. "Not part of your graph. Just standing near it."

Someone draws the line to him in Book III. He is not present for it.

* Wick in Book II (from the superseded outline)


The favor-graph surfaces: hundreds of debts across three nations, never called.
Leina finally believes she has him modeled — he is accumulating leverage — and
she is wrong, and she acts on being right, and people die for it.

Then the peak of the love/hate: in the wreckage she does the thing she has never
done, asks him a direct question, and requires a true answer in payment of the
unnamed favor from Book I. He tells her the truth about Alistair's debt. She
would rather he had lied. She banishes him and he goes without argument, which
is the only time he has ever done exactly what she asked.
