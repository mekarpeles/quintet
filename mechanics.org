#+TITLE: Quintet — The Curse
#+SUBTITLE: Rebuilt 2026-09-17. Supersedes all earlier versions (pump / switch / book-as-anchor / range-irrelevant).

* Physics

Soul speaking is direct intervention in biology. In our world you intervene
chemically, optically, electrically; on Orbis there is another channel. Like all
of those it is neutral — it heals or harms depending on what is done with it.

Oracle stone focuses and amplifies the channel.

*Optional, and independently useful:* the true Manual's cover boards or spine
contain oracle stone, which is why *this* copy does things the household copies
cannot. Four centuries of annotators were writing in the margins of an
instrument. It is also part of why a twelve-year-old could do anything at all in
that kitchen — she was holding the amplifier. (This is no longer load-bearing;
nothing depends on it.)

* Two procedures, not one

This is the distinction everything rests on, and it was missing.

*The Reds do REPAIR.* A skilled practitioner goes into his chest and patches —
slows the failure, shores up what is collapsing, withdraws. It is their work, spent
out of their own vitality (hence the shaking hands in scene 004), left behind in
him, and it degrades. Hence: come back in twenty-eight days.

*Leina did SUBSTITUTION.* She did not patch his heart. She routed the deficit onto
herself. His heart is still failing exactly as fast as it always was — she is
paying the difference, continuously, out of her own body.

** Why an untrained twelve-year-old outperforms their professionals

It is *not* skill. The Reds know substitution perfectly well. They will not use it,
because it costs a whole person permanently, and they are not spending a
practitioner to keep one assassin alive. The Manual documents it as a surgical
measure: hours, supervised, released at the end.

Leina's genius is not power. It is **willingness.** She did the thing nobody does,
to herself, because the alternative was a knife.

* What Leina did

The Manual contains a genuine procedure: sustaining a failing organ by tying it
to a donor. Temporary, supervised, and it has a *closing step* — what the
practitioner does at the end to release the patient.

She performed it in ninety seconds with a knife at her throat, having only ever
read it.

**She wrote the binding and never wrote the ending.**

A twelve-year-old's error, permanent, and specifically a *literacy* failure: she
knew the words and not the grammar.

* Why it cannot be undone

*Encoded and substrate are not in conflict — resolve them like this:* the encoding
is the **plumbing**, a fixed self-sustaining rule with her signature on it. What
moves through the plumbing is her vitality. The pipe is permanent; the flow depends
on the reservoir. That is why it cannot be let go of, and also why her condition
governs everything.

It is *encoded*, not held. She is not maintaining a flow — she set a rule with
her signature on it. Self-sustaining, and hard to reverse in the way encryption is
hard to break. Others can fight its effects (a Red Soul Speaker could labour to
keep Ardovus alive by other means) but that is their effort against hers, and they
do not understand what she wrote.

*Two different operations, and keep them separate:*

- **Killing him** is possible. She can damage the link with time and access.
- **Releasing him** is not. *And the reason is crueller than "no release was ever
  written."* The documented closing step exists and she can read it — but it works
  on a fresh binding you are still actively holding. Hers set and hardened. So she
  has the instructions, they are right there in the book, and they do not work. She
  can sit with the words that should free them both and watch them fail.

* The conditions

1. If he kills her, he unravels.
2. If her living force is out of range of him, he unravels.
3. Her living force is the *substrate*. See below — this is the important one.

** It is TIME, not distance (mek, 2026-09-17)

The constraint is *time since contact.* Distance matters only because distance
eats the clock — go a week's ride out and two weeks of the window are spent on
travel. Effective range is half the window.

*What resets it is a soak, not a touch.* He must be near her for a sustained
period. Returning is not a moment, it is a stay. Forced cohabitation, repeatedly,
for years, with the person he most wants to escape.

*The window is a function of her condition.* Rested, fed, unharassed: a long rope.
Sick, exhausted, miserable: a short one. So he cannot plan his own movements
without managing her wellbeing, and before every departure he has to invest in her
— feed her up, settle her — out of pure self-interest.

Which hands her the quietest leverage in the books: she sets the length of his rope
by how well she is, she controls how well she is, and **he can never prove she is
doing it on purpose.**

*And the irony is the point.* He has swapped one maintenance schedule for another.
Eleven years of thirty-day extensions from the Reds, escaped into a window granted
by a twelve-year-old. He is psychologically pre-fitted for it. He knows exactly how
to be someone's line item.

*He learns the window the only way available:* by going too far, once, and having
to get back.

It is a gradient, not a fence — symptoms begin well before the limit — so finding
it takes months of dangerous trial, and he retains real operational latitude. He
can push past comfortable and pay for it. Which is what makes Book II possible.

* PILLAR ONE — he does not need her alive. He needs her *well*.

Her living force is the substrate, so the supply is proportional to her condition.
Sick, starved, caged, exhausted, despairing: she supplies less, and his heart gets
worse in direct measure.

Consequences, all of them good:

- *Imprisonment fails.* A cellar keeps her alive and degrades her, which degrades
  him. This is the answer to "why doesn't he just cage her," which the previous
  version had no answer for.
- *Neglect fails.* He has to feed her, keep her warm, let her sleep.
- *Torture is self-limiting.* He can hurt her in bursts — and he does, early, to
  establish that the curse bought her survival and not safety — but the bill
  arrives, which caps his only real weapon and forces him off it.
- *He has to keep her from despair.* An assassin professionally obliged to keep a
  twelve-year-old in tolerable spirits, and not one degree of it is kindness.
- *The asymmetry stays correct.* She needs him alive; he needs her well. Well is
  much harder than alive. She is doing more work, in every sense.

* PILLAR TWO — the book survives because he wants out

Not because it anchors anything. Because he wants to be free of her, and freedom
requires the closing step she never wrote, and if that release exists anywhere on
Orbis it is in four hundred years of marginalia in that book.

And only she can read it well enough to find it.

So he is not guarding the girl who keeps him alive. **He is funding a research
programme.** She is the only researcher. The deliverable is his own freedom, and
she sets the pace entirely.

He is her sponsor and her prisoner at once, dependent on the goodwill of the
person he most wants to escape.

*And it handles the Reds without assertion:* send the book north and they may
derive the release themselves — at which point they own him outright, more
completely than the lease ever did. So he can never let it go, and neither can she,
for different reasons.

* Why nobody can move

| Ardovus could | Result |
|---------------+--------|
| Kill her | He unravels. |
| Leave | Out of range. He unravels. |
| Cage or break her | Degrades the substrate. His heart worsens. |
| Deliver her and the book to the Reds | They experiment on her, which kills him — and the instant the book lands his value as the finder is spent, and he has watched eleven years of what the Reds do with assets that have finished producing. |
| Let the Reds have the book alone | They may find the release. Then they own him permanently. |
| Keep her well, keep the book, keep both from the Reds, and put her to work on the release | **The only play.** |

| Leina could | Result |
|-------------+--------|
| Kill him | Possible, with time. Costs her permanent capacity she cannot spare. |
| Run out of range | Kills him — and loses the best living source on the Reds. |
| Destroy the book | Destroys the only real copy in the world, which four centuries of hanged people died carrying. |
| Stay well, keep the book, work the problem slowly | **The only play.** |

They want the same three things for entirely different reasons and neither can
defect.

* What stays live inside the lock

- He can hurt her in bursts and take the feedback, because he has two decades of
  professional tolerance and she is twelve. Self-limiting, but real, and it is how
  the first weeks go.
- She can out-think him, and does, one chip at a time.
- And the research is the whole relationship: every year she does not solve it is a
  year he stays, and he can never be sure whether she is slow or unwilling.

* Open

- Does she ever find the release? And if she does, does she give it to him?
- What is he doing during Book II? Answerable now: operating inside the radius as
  her outside man — violence and Red-side intelligence, the things she cannot do —
  while she runs the Academy. The arrangement matures into a division of labour.

* Deeper mechanism, and the holes (analysis 2026-09-18)

** Why she is not dead or crippled within a year

He needs the *deficit* covered, not his whole heart run — a leaking roof needs a
bucket, not a new house. It also explains her long-established frailty.

But the real answer is dynamic: *his condition worsens across the series while her
capacity grows.* Twelve to seventeen, plus genuine training. For a long while it is
roughly a wash. Then it stops being one, and that is Book III's clock.

** Can she partially damage the link?

No. If she could, she would have a dial and the whole "no switch" design collapses.
It is a *tear, not a valve.* You cannot half-tear; any damage propagates. All or
nothing, and expensive.

** What "unravel" actually means

Not "his heart stops." The substitution stops covering, and eleven years of
deferred failure arrive at once. Everything the Reds' patchwork was holding back
becomes present tense within days.

** He can never be treated by a Red practitioner again

The first one who goes into his chest finds her signature. Treatment is now
exposure, permanently. This locks him harder than any of the other conditions.

** The Reds could industrialise this — and this is a plot, not a hole

They do not use substitution because it costs a person. The Protector spends
people. The instant the Reds learn that a permanent substitution *holds*, they have
unlimited maintenance and a use for prisoners nobody has thought of yet.

Leina's improvisation is a technology, and she is carrying it around in a man's
chest.

** THE BIG ONE — her state comes through

If her vitality flows to him, so does her condition. That is what makes "he needs
her well" work at all. Which means he *feels* her: pain, exhaustion, fear, despair.

Leina has spent her entire life being unreadable. It is the core of everything she
is and everything she was made into.

She is now permanently, involuntarily legible to exactly one person, and it is the
man who killed her mother.

*The channels are asymmetric.* She gets his **body** — she can hear a failing
heart. He gets her **state.** His is far more intimate. Neither chose it. It cannot
be switched off.

This is the deepest thing in the design and it had not been written down anywhere
before this entry.

* The dynamic on the page (2026-09-21, settled)

- *Ardovus does not comment.* He is Strider: quiet, deadly, introspective, lets
  nobody in. He does not narrate his sensations or interrogate a child. Most days
  he does not speak. Every line he does say is therefore enormous.
- *So the leak is discovered by her, not disclosed by him.* He acts on things he
  should not know — moves them early, stops before she says she needs to, kills a
  man she had not finished being afraid of — and she assembles it over weeks,
  alone, and can never confirm it.
- *She gives him true things to hang readings on.* She cannot hide the signal, so
  she supplies innocent causes for it. But never unprompted: offering an
  explanation nobody asked for tells a professional that something needs
  explaining.
- *He resists, constantly.* He gets more signal than he shows and spends every
  waking hour on three things at once — not leaking, not being read, modelling her.
- *Leina has the upper hand and the exhaustion is his.* He worked in bursts his
  whole career; this is continuous, with no off-duty and no end date. She was built
  for it at five and finds it restful — she has never had a room where she didn't
  have to do this. He is losing a war of attrition to a child who does not
  experience it as a war.
- *She does not underestimate him; she has not finished.* Leina carries partial
  models correctly labelled as partial, never wrong ones. The dictionary has a line
  that stays open for years: /What he gets from it — incomplete. More than he
  shows. Have not established how much./ Never crossed out, never guessed at.
- *Which means he is not deceiving her.* He is fighting a delaying action against
  someone who is definitely going to arrive, and he has known it since about month
  three. That is what makes it exhausting rather than merely difficult.
